Real-time glass, in five terms.

Iridescence — Wave interference within microscopic thin films generates fluid chromatic shifts that shimmer across the obsidian surface as viewing angles tilt toward incident illumination.

Index of refraction — Governs Snell's law boundary bending, determining how light decelerates upon entering the dense smoked glass matrix and how sharply glancing rays reflect off polished bevels.

Dispersion — Spectral separation of polychromatic light into constituent wavelengths, splitting specular highlights into subtle prismatic fringing across high-curvature rounded contours.

Transmission — The selective absorption and volumetric transit of photons through the physical medium, rendering deep smoky charcoal tones that soften internal refractions.

Roughness — Microfacet distribution across the outer boundary layer, balancing mirror-like specular highlights against velvety diffuse light scattering on dark glass faces.
